<p class="eyebrow">METRONOME GUIDE</p><h1>Chord changes<br><em>without losing the beat.</em></h1>
<section class="story"><p class="eyebrow">WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO</p><h2>You know all the chords, but the song stops every time you switch.</h2><p>The most common wall in guitar practice is chord changes. While your fingers hunt for the next chord, the beat stretches, and then it speeds up again in the easy parts. You don't notice alone, but it shows the moment you play with a drummer.</p><p>DailyDrum drum studio teaches the drummers who are responsible for a band's time every week. We built TEMPO so you can use that same reference beat for guitar practice.</p><ul><li>8th and 16th subdivisions matched to your strumming pattern</li><li>Triplet subdivision and drum machine presets for shuffle and swing songs</li><li>Save BPM per practice song, and use the Automator to reach the original speed</li></ul>{{BRAND}}</section>
<h2>Guitar metronome practice in 3 steps</h2><h3>1. Timing your chord changes</h3><p>At BPM 60 in 4/4, change chords every bar. The first beat of each new chord must land exactly on the click. Once that works, change every two beats, then every beat.</p><h3>2. Strumming</h3><p>Match down and up strokes to an 8th-note subdivision click. Keep your wrist moving in 8ths without stopping, and on beats you don't play, let the hand pass without touching the strings. Practice 16-beat strumming with the 16th subdivision.</p><h3>3. Solos and speed</h3><p>For fast electric guitar runs, start at 60% of your target and raise the BPM by 4–5 after three clean passes in a row. The Automator can raise the tempo for you.</p><h2>For ukulele</h2><p>The ukulele is quiet and light, so the click easily sticks out. Practicing with a soft sound like Gentle at a low volume keeps long sessions comfortable.</p>

##### how-to-use-metronome
TITLE: How to Use and Read a Metronome | The basics of playing in time
DESC: What do the numbers on a metronome mean, and how do you play along with the click? From reading a metronome to keeping time, DailyDrum, a one-on-one drum studio, explains from the basics.
CRUMB: How to use a metronome
---
<p class="eyebrow">METRONOME GUIDE</p><h1>How to use a metronome,<br><em>riding on top of the click.</em></h1>
<section class="story"><p class="eyebrow">WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO</p><h2>"Practice with a metronome this week." But how?</h2><p>Your teacher gave you homework and you turned on the metronome, but you're so busy chasing the click that you can't actually play. It feels like the click is chasing you, and if you miss a single beat you can't tell where beat one is.</p><p>In drum lessons, too, learning to use a metronome is the first step. DailyDrum, a one-on-one drum studio, put that first step into TEMPO so you can follow it alone.</p><ul><li>Accent beat one so you never lose your place in the bar</li><li>Turn on subdivisions to hear the space between beats</li><li>Save per song to practice at exactly the BPM your teacher set</li></ul>{{BRAND}}</section>
<h2>How to read a metronome</h2><ul><li><strong>The big number (BPM)</strong> · Beats per minute. At 60 it sounds once per second.</li><li><strong>Time signature (4/4 etc.)</strong> · How many beats are in one bar.</li><li><strong>Subdivision</strong> · How many parts each beat is split into (8ths, 16ths, triplets).</li><li><strong>Accent</strong> · Which beats sound louder or with a different sound.</li></ul><h2>5 steps to playing in time</h2><ol><li>Turn on the click, and before playing, <strong>count one bar out loud</strong> (one, two, three, four)</li><li><strong>Tap the beat</strong> with your foot or hand</li><li>Play <strong>one note at a time</strong> and listen for whether it lines up exactly with the click</li><li>If the click "disappears" under your playing, you're right on it</li><li>If you slip, don't stop; <strong>rejoin on the next beat one</strong></li></ol><h2>The most common mistakes</h2><ul><li>Setting the click so loud you can't hear yourself</li><li>Starting at the original song's speed</li><li>Starting over from the top every time you slip (practicing rejoining on beat one matters more)</li></ul><h2>FAQ</h2><h3>I keep drifting away from the click.</h3><p>Lower the BPM by 10–20 and practice with a subdivision on so you hear between the beats. If you can lock in at a slow tempo, you can lock in at a fast one.</p>
