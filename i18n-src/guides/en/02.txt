Keep the start and end (length) of each note even.</li><li><strong>8th-note root picking</strong> · Turn on the 8th subdivision and keep your picking strength even.</li><li><strong>Click on 2 and 4</strong> · Use per-beat mute so only 2 and 4 sound, and hold the groove.</li><li><strong>Jam with the drum machine</strong> · Play a basic beat and lock your bass to where the kick lands.</li></ol><h2>Where bassists rush</h2><ul><li>The first note of the bar after a fill</li><li>Lines with rests (it's easy to count rests short)</li><li>Slap and 16th-note passages</li></ul><div class="box"><h3>TEMPO drum machine</h3><ul><li>4 tracks: kick, snare, hi-hat, ride</li><li>From basic beats to shuffle, jazz and bossa nova presets</li><li>16th swing, tempo control, loop playback</li></ul><p>Tap the DailyDrum logo on the Tempo tab 8 times quickly to open it.</p></div>

<p class="eyebrow">METRONOME GUIDE</p><h1>Measure the BPM, set it,<br><em>and save it.</em></h1>
<section class="story"><p class="eyebrow">WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO</p><h2>"What BPM is this song?" The answer is always "about this fast."</h2><p>If you don't know the exact BPM of the song you're practicing, you set the speed by feel every time, and yesterday's practice speed differs from today's. In a band, everyone remembers a different tempo, so you're off from the very first beat.</p><p>A song's tempo should be set by number, not by feel. To help anyone keep that habit, DailyDrum, a one-on-one drum studio, put measuring, setting and saving a BPM into one flow on one screen in TEMPO.</p><ul><li>Measure BPM by tapping with your chosen click sound</li><li>Save BPM, time signature and subdivision per song</li><li>TEMPO ANCHOR: re-locks the beat while keeping the sound and downbeat accent</li></ul>{{BRAND}}</section>
<h2>How to measure BPM (TAP)</h2><ol><li>Listen to the original song and tap TAP along with the beat several times</li><li>Round off the displayed BPM (e.g. 118.6 → 119 or 120)</li><li>Save it in List with the song name</li></ol><h2>How to use tempo control</h2><ul><li>For practice: start lower, at 60–70% of the original</li><li>To prepare for the stage: also try 5–10 faster than the original</li><li>Quick fixes during a live set: change the BPM right on the Live screen</li></ul><h2>Using it as a click track</h2><p>When you need a click track for recording or rehearsal, you can load the BPM, meter and count-in saved for each song and start right away. It's a click and count feature; it does not play backing tracks.</p><p>For what BPM means and BPM by tempo marking, see the <a href="/bpm/">BPM guide</a>.</p>
