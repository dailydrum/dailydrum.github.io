<p class="eyebrow">METRONOME GUIDE</p><h1>The metronome,<br><em>a machine that measures the beat.</em></h1>
<section class="story"><p class="eyebrow">WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO</p><h2>That swinging weight you first saw in music class: did you know its name?</h2><p>Almost everyone has seen a metronome, but few people know what the name means or how it works. Once you understand how it works, why you should practice with one makes sense naturally.</p><p>Understanding the mechanism changes how you practice. DailyDrum teaches everyone from children to adults, and built TEMPO as a metronome app anyone can use easily.</p><ul><li>The same principle as a wind-up metronome, with flexible settings</li><li>BPM saved per song, adjustable meter, subdivision and accents</li><li>A screen easy enough for children and adults alike</li></ul>{{BRAND}}</section>
<h2>What "metronome" means</h2><p>The word metronome comes from Greek: <em>metron</em>, meaning "measure," and <em>nomos</em>, meaning "rule" or "law." Literally, it's "the rule that measures the beat."</p><h2>How a wind-up metronome works</h2><p>A wind-up metronome uses a swinging pendulum. Slide the weight on the rod up and the pendulum swings slowly; slide it down and it swings fast. Each time the pendulum passes, it makes a "tick" to mark the beat. The numbers on the rod are beats per minute: BPM.</p><h2>Digital metronomes and metronome apps</h2><p>Digital metronomes and apps use electronic signals instead of a pendulum to make a sound at fixed intervals. That makes it easy to change the time signature, split the beat, or accent only certain beats.</p><p>For how metronome numbers relate to tempo markings, see the <a href="/bpm/">BPM guide</a>.</p>

<section class="story"><p class="eyebrow">WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO</p><h2>"I have no rhythm." <h2>The real causes of bad timing</h2><ul><li>Only "hearing" the beat, not "feeling" it in your body</li><li>Not counting between beats (subdivisions)</li><li>Counting rests short</li><li>Breathing and tempo both speed up when you're nervous</li></ul><h2>A 4-week rhythm training plan</h2><h3>Week 1 · Count the beat with your body</h3><p>Turn on the click at BPM 60–70, step on the beat with your foot, and count "one, two, three, four" out loud. Five minutes a day without an instrument is enough.</p><h3>Week 2 · Split the beat</h3><p>Turn on the 8th subdivision and clap 8th notes. Once that's comfortable, switch to 16ths and triplets.</p><h3>Week 3 · Click on 2 and 4</h3><p>Use per-beat mute so the click sounds only on 2 and 4, and fill in 1 and 3 yourself. You'll develop the backbeat feel of popular music.</p><h3>Week 4 · Once per bar</h3><p>Leave the click only on beat one and mute the rest. When you can fill the three empty beats accurately, you can hold the tempo without a click.</p><div class="box"><h3>Why use TEMPO as a rhythm practice app</h3><ul><li>Per-beat accent and mute let you do the 4-week plan exactly as written</li><li>Pick whichever of Digital, Gentle or Acoustic is comfortable over long sessions</li><li>Save per song to pick up where you left off each day</li></ul></div><h2>FAQ</h2><h3>Can adults fix their timing too?</h3><p>Yes.
