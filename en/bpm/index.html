The big number on a metronome screen and the ♩= 120 above a score say the same thing. Once you understand BPM, you can set the practice speed for any song yourself.

What is BPM?

BPM (Beats Per Minute) is the number of beats in one minute. BPM 60 is one beat per second; BPM 120 is two beats per second. The number on your metronome is this BPM, and the higher it is, the faster it goes.

If a score says ♩ = 90, it means "a quarter note is one beat, 90 times per minute." Songs in 6/8 are sometimes marked with a dotted quarter, like ♩. = 60, so check which note value counts as one beat.

Tempo marking BPM chart

Tempo markings describe a feeling rather than an exact number, so ranges vary a little between sources. Below are commonly used approximate ranges.

Common BPM by genre

Every song is different, but this helps you get a feel. Ballads often sit around 60–80, mid-tempo pop and worship 80–110, dance and up-tempo pop 110–130, rock 110–160, and punk and metal 160 and above. The best way to get the exact value is to measure it yourself while listening to the original.

How to choose your practice BPM

  1. Find the original BPM. If you don't know it, tap the screen along with the beat (TAP) while listening to get a rough BPM.
  2. Check whether you can play it without mistakes at 60–70% of the original BPM.
  3. When you play it cleanly several times in a row, raise it by 2–5 BPM.
  4. Once you reach the original BPM, also try playing 5–10 faster. It gives you room to spare when nerves kick in on stage.
