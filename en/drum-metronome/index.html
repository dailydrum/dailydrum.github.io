The drummer is the band's clock. If the guitar wobbles a bit, the song goes on; if the drums wobble, everyone does. That's why a metronome should always be part of drum practice.

First, make the click audible

Drums are loud, and a click from a phone speaker gets buried fast.

  • Use earphones or headphones: With isolating earphones or headphones the click is clear even at low volume. It's good hearing protection, too.
  • Choose the sound: Pick a tone that doesn't disappear under cymbals and snare. Some people hear a sharp digital sound best; others prefer something softer.
  • Accent the first beat: Give beat one a different sound or more volume so you never lose your place in the bar.

Step-by-step metronome practice for drums

1. Basic 8-beat groove

Start at BPM 70–80 with 8th notes on the hi-hat, snare on 2 and 4, and kick on 1 and 3. If the click seems to "disappear" behind the hi-hat, you're right on it.

2. Sharpen accuracy with 16th subdivisions

For 16-beat grooves and fast kick patterns, practice with the 16th-note subdivision on. With the space between beats filled in, you hear right away where you're pulling ahead.

3. Landing on beat one after a fill

Fills are where drummers rush the most. Repeat "3 bars of groove + 1 bar of fill," and check that the crash and kick on the first beat after the fill land exactly with the click.

4. Click only on 2 and 4

Once you're comfortable, make the click sound only on beats 2 and 4. Playing the snare right on the click improves your backbeat feel, and you fill in 1 and 3 yourself.

5. Shuffle and swing

Practicing shuffle with a triplet subdivision click locks in the spacing between the first and third notes. For swing feel, practice with a drum machine whose swing amount you can adjust, and your ear picks it up quickly.

What we often tell our drum students

"Playing evenly comes before playing fast." A groove that doesn't waver at slow tempos is the shortest path to playing fast songs. If you write the BPM in your practice log each day, a month later you'll see in numbers how far you've come.
