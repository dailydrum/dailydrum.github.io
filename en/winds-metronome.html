METRONOME GUIDE

Even when you're short of breath,
don't come up short on tempo.

WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO

At the end of a breathless phrase, everyone rushes a little.

On wind instruments, breath is time. When you run out of air at the end of a long phrase, you clip the note and rush the next one; where you breathe, the beat stretches.

TEMPO, made by DailyDrum, helps you practice counting even the beats where you breathe. With a downbeat accent and subdivision clicks, you always know which beat of the phrase you're on.

  • Keep your place in the bar through long phrases with a downbeat accent
  • Click tone and volume that don't get lost under your horn
  • Save BPM per song to learn the ensemble tempo in advance

TEMPO is a metronome app made by DailyDrum, a drum studio in Anyang (Pyeongchon), South Korea, where every lesson, from children to adults and church musicians, is taught one-on-one.

Metronome practice for winds

  1. Long tones · At BPM 60, hold 4- and 8-beat long tones. Line up the start and end of each note with the click.
  2. Plan your breaths · Mark where to breathe in the music, and count up to that beat with the click.
  3. Tonguing · Even out single and double tonguing to 8th and 16th subdivisions.
  4. Pieces · Start at 70% of the target tempo and raise it with the Automator.

One-line tips by instrument

  • Saxophone · For swing tunes, internalize the 8th-note swing ratio with a triplet subdivision
  • Flute · For fast passages, get your fingers right to the 16th subdivision first
  • Clarinet · Isolate register changes and take them slowly
  • Trumpet · Check the accented beat so you don't rush right before high notes
