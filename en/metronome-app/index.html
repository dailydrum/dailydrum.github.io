If you practice daily and also rehearse or perform, an app ends up being the most practical. So what should you compare between apps?

7 things to check when choosing a metronome app

1. Is the click clear to your ears?

A click that gets buried under the drums is useless. Check whether you can change the sound and adjust the downbeat accent separately.

2. Are time signatures and subdivisions flexible?

An app that only does 4/4 hits its limit fast. Look for meters like 3/4 and 6/8, 8th, 16th and triplet subdivisions, and per-beat accent or mute.

3. Can you save a BPM for each song?

As your song list grows, re-setting the BPM every time becomes the biggest hassle. A list that saves song names with BPM and meter gets you practicing faster.

4. Can you use it on stage?

During a show there's no time to hunt for tiny buttons. Check for a setlist you can step through in order, large controls and a count-in.

5. Are there tools for pushing your tempo up?

A feature that raises the tempo gradually from slow to your target BPM means you don't have to remember "where did I get to today?"

6. Where do the ads appear?

Most free apps have ads. What matters is where. Make sure ads don't cover the screen you look at while playing.

7. Is the paid upgrade a subscription or a one-time purchase?

A metronome is a tool you use for years, so a monthly subscription versus a one-time purchase makes a big difference in total cost.

Bottom line: how to choose

If you only practice alone, an app with clear sounds, subdivisions and accents is enough. For lessons or exam prep, look for per-song saving and tempo-increase tools. For bands, gigs and worship teams, choose an app with setlists, a big screen and count-ins. Either way, the surest comparison is to try it free first and spend one week checking whether it fits your practice habits.
