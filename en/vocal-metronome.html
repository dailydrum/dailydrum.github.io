METRONOME GUIDE

As important as pitch:
the timing of a song.

WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO

The pitch was right, but you came in half a beat before the band.

Singers spend a lot of time on pitch, but on stage, timing is what shows first. The first line after the intro, lyrics on the off-beat, and the next line after a long note are where it wavers.

DailyDrum drum studio teaches how to feel the beat in your body. TEMPO's voice count and subdivision clicks work just as well for learning by ear where a vocal line comes in.

  • Practice your first entrance with the voice count-in
  • Find where syncopated lyrics fall with 8th and 16th subdivisions
  • Save BPM per song to practice timing even without accompaniment

TEMPO is a metronome app made by DailyDrum, a drum studio in Anyang (Pyeongchon), South Korea, where every lesson, from children to adults and church musicians, is taught one-on-one.

Timing practice for singers

  1. Read the lyric rhythm · Speak the lyrics in rhythm to the click, without pitch
  2. Syncopated lyrics · Turn on the 8th subdivision and mark where lyrics fall on the "and"
  3. Entrances · Listen to the count-in and start the first line exactly
  4. After long notes · Hold long notes for their full number of beats before moving on

For rap practice

Fit your lyrics to a 16th subdivision click. For fast verses, lower the BPM below the target to clean up your diction first, then use the Automator to bring it up to the original speed.
