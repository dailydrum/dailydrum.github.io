WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO

You know all the chords, but the song stops every time you switch.

The most common wall in guitar practice is chord changes. While your fingers hunt for the next chord, the beat stretches, and then it speeds up again in the easy parts. You don't notice alone, but it shows the moment you play with a drummer.

DailyDrum drum studio teaches the drummers who are responsible for a band's time every week. We built TEMPO so you can use that same reference beat for guitar practice.

  • 8th and 16th subdivisions matched to your strumming pattern
  • Triplet subdivision and drum machine presets for shuffle and swing songs
  • Save BPM per practice song, and use the Automator to reach the original speed

Guitar metronome practice in 3 steps

1. Timing your chord changes

At BPM 60 in 4/4, change chords every bar. The first beat of each new chord must land exactly on the click. Once that works, change every two beats, then every beat.

2. Strumming

Match down and up strokes to an 8th-note subdivision click. Keep your wrist moving in 8ths without stopping, and on beats you don't play, let the hand pass without touching the strings. Practice 16-beat strumming with the 16th subdivision.

3. Solos and speed

For fast electric guitar runs, start at 60% of your target and raise the BPM by 4–5 after three clean passes in a row. The Automator can raise the tempo for you.

For ukulele

The ukulele is quiet and light, so the click easily sticks out. Practicing with a soft sound like Gentle at a low volume keeps long sessions comfortable.
