WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO

Fast in the easy bars, slow in the hard ones. Everyone's piano habit.

When you practice piano alone, your hands race through the familiar parts and slow down on their own in the hard spots. You may not notice while playing, but listeners hear an uneven piece.

DailyDrum also teaches church accompanists. So accompanists can stay on the same beat as the worship team, TEMPO saves BPM and count-in for each song.

  • BPM and time signature saved per song, exactly as set in your lesson
  • Clicks like Gentle and Acoustic that blend with the piano sound
  • Automator to go from a slow practice tempo to the marked tempo

TEMPO is a metronome app made by DailyDrum, a drum studio in Anyang (Pyeongchon), South Korea, where every lesson, from children to adults and church musicians, is taught one-on-one.

Piano metronome practice order

  1. Isolate the hard bars and practice them at a BPM you can play comfortably
  2. Add one bar before and after at the same BPM
  3. Play through the whole piece once at that BPM
  4. Raise by 2–5 BPM at a time until you reach the tempo marking

Tempo markings and BPM

Markings like Andante, Moderato and Allegro only give a rough range. Use the tempo marking BPM chart to choose a BPM that suits the character of the piece.

For church accompanists

  • When you get the setlist, save BPM, meter and count-in for each song in List
  • Hearing the same click as the worship leader keeps the tempo from wavering
  • Handle transitions between songs with the voice count-in on the Live screen
