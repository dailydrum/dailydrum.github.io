WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO

Bands and worship teams need many people riding the same beat. If every song's BPM is set by feel, and nobody is sure who starts the next song or how, the tempo wobbles easily on stage.

It was fine in rehearsal, but on stage the songs crept faster. When one song ends, it's awkward every time to decide who kicks off the next tempo and how. These are the scenes bands and worship teams run into most.

1. Start with a BPM for every song

Most team tempo problems begin with deciding "what tempo are we doing this one?" by feel, every time. Before practice, write down these four things for each song.

  • BPM · Based on the original, adjusted to your team's level and the mood
  • Time signature · 4/4, 6/8, 3/4 and so on
  • Subdivision · Does it feel like 8ths, 16ths or a shuffle?
  • Count-in · How many beats (or bars) before you come in

For worship teams, simply noting this information whenever you receive the setlist cuts rehearsal time dramatically.

2. Save songs in setlist order

If you put songs in a list in the order of the show or service, you never have to re-enter BPMs when the song changes. When you play the same song next month, just load it again.

3. Who hears the click, and how

  • Only the drummer: The most common setup. If the drummer stays steady, the whole team follows.
  • The whole team on in-ears: The most stable tempo, but check your monitor routing so the click never leaks into the house or congregation speakers.
  • Only the leader: The worship leader or bandleader hears the click and leads the team with a count.

4. Smooth transitions between songs

The shakiest moment is jumping straight into the next song's tempo after one ends. A spoken count-in for the next song lets everyone start on the same beat. If two songs with very different tempos follow each other, giving the count-in one extra bar also helps.

Managing team tempo with TEMPO

  • List: Save BPM, time signature, subdivision and count-in per song, organized in folders. Send it as a file and teammates import it as a new list (no sign-up, not real-time sync).
  • Live: Move to the next song with large controls and your saved voice count-in gets you ready. Quick BPM fixes right on screen. The Live screen has no ads.
  • TEMPO ANCHOR: Re-locks the beat while keeping your chosen sound and downbeat accent.
  • Playback (coming in iOS 2.5.3): Save a song's sections, like Intro, Verse and Chorus, with their bar counts, and get a voice cue before the next part. It gives clicks and section cues; it does not play backing tracks.

Rehearsal checklist

  1. Did you share each song's BPM, meter and count-in before rehearsal?
  2. Did you decide who hears the click and how (speaker or in-ear)?
  3. Did you check that the click doesn't leak to the audience or congregation?
  4. Did you run the transitions between songs with very different tempos on their own?
  5. Did you mark the songs that tend to speed up on stage (climaxes, endings)?
