Split the beat,

WHY DAILYDRUM TEMPO

Play 16ths to a quarter-note click, and the space between beats is left to chance.

Rhythm suddenly gets hard the moment four or three notes fit inside one beat. The click sounds once per beat, but you have to fill the notes in between on your own.

The shortcut for 16ths and triplets is to practice first with a "split click." TEMPO, made by DailyDrum, lets you save that subdivision click for every song.

  • 8th, 16th and triplet subdivision clicks
  • Per-beat accents to tell the first note and off-beats apart by sound
  • Practice while hearing patterns on the drum machine's 16th/triplet grid

16th-note practice

  1. Turn on the 16th subdivision and start at BPM 60
  2. Count the four notes out loud: "one-e-and-a"
  3. Play the four 16ths evenly on hi-hat or by clapping
  4. When it's even four times in a row, raise the BPM by 4–5

Triplet practice

Triplets divide one beat into three. Turn on the triplet subdivision click and count "one-trip-let." The key is not rushing the third note into the next beat. A shuffle rhythm plays just the first and third notes of these triplets.

Off-beat practice

Off-beats are notes that fall between beats (on the "and"). Turn on the 8th subdivision and start by clapping only on the "and." Once comfortable, reduce the beat click and try holding the tempo with off-beats alone.
